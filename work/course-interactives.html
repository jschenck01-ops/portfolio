Online Courses & Marketing EdTech · Instructional Design

Courses you do, not just watch.

Five levels of accessible, self-contained interactivity, built into every course.

Jeff Schenck · Chief Brand Strategist & Fractional CMO

A lecture tells you something. An activity makes you do it, and doing it is what makes it stick.

Every course ships with interactive pieces built the same way the lectures are: accessible, on-brand, and self-contained. They run in the browser with no plugins, and they work with a keyboard and a screen reader, not just a mouse.

Five levels of interactivity

Not every lesson needs a simulation, and not every concept survives a slideshow. The catalog is built across five complexity levels, matched to what each moment is trying to teach. Below is a live proof of concept for each, running exactly as a student would use it. Try one.

Level 1

Basic Content Presentation

Static or lightly interactive content the student moves through at their own pace: tabbed panels, clickable reveals, hotspots, flip cards. The job is to make reading feel like exploring.Proof of concept · Tabbed lesson object, arrow-key navigable.

Foundational Interactives

Intermediate Interactives

Multi-step, paced experiences with guidance built in: auto-advancing walkthroughs, carousels, and knowledge checks that unfold a concept one beat at a time.Proof of concept · Auto-advancing walkthrough with play, pause, and a check.

Level 4

Advanced Interactive
