Anatomy of a Course People Finish

Before a single interaction, the content itself has to earn the next click. Move through the four parts of a lesson that keeps a student going.

Open the loop

The Hook

Attention is not owed, it is borrowed. The first fifteen seconds open a question the student now needs answered. Not a summary of what is coming, a reason to stay.

A pope once dug up his dead predecessor and put the corpse on trial. By the end of this lesson you will know why, and what it tells us about power.

Give it a shape

The Frame

People remember structure, not sentences. The frame tells the student where they are and how many steps remain, so nothing feels endless.

Three beats, named up front: what happened, why it happened, what it means now. The map is the lesson before the lesson.

Deliver the shift

The Turn

Every lesson worth finishing has one moment where the student's understanding tilts. Everything before it was setup. The turn is the point you were paying for.

The trial was not madness. It was a legal maneuver to erase a rival's decrees, a lesson in how institutions rewrite their own history.

Close the loop

The Payoff

The hook made a promise. The payoff keeps it, out loud, so the student feels the click of a thing completed and reaches for the next one.

Completion is a feeling before it is a metric. Give it to them on purpose, every single lesson, and the numbers follow.
